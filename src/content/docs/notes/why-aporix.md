Aporix started as a place to make engineering thinking public.

The name comes from aporia: a state of uncertainty, contradiction, or unresolved questioning.

I do not want this site to be a collection of polished opinions. I want it to be a working notebook: a place where unclear problems are slowly turned into structured models.

## Why This Exists

Many engineering problems do not begin with clarity. They begin with strange behavior:

- a model output changes when batch size changes
- a service becomes unstable only under certain traffic patterns
- a queue stops consuming even though the process is alive
- a protocol looks simple until timeout, retry, and compatibility appear
- an agent seems powerful until file access, shell execution, and rollback become necessary

These situations are confusing. But the confusion itself is valuable. It points to a missing model.

Aporix exists to capture that moment and turn it into something reusable.

## Core Line

**Turn confusion into systems.**
